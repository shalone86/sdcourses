# How These Lessons Are Made

Every lesson on this site starts with a person and ends with a person. In between, I use an AI assistant (Claude, made by Anthropic) for much of the legwork. Here is exactly who does what.

## What I do

- **Choose the topic.** Every course and lesson exists because I decided it was worth teaching.
- **Choose the sources and the focus.** I decide which passages from Scripture, the Catechism, and other trusted sources each lesson is built on, and which points are worth your time.
- **Write the introduction and closing** of every lesson, and edit the rest until it says what I mean, the way I would say it.
- **Narrate every video** in my own voice.
- **Review and approve** every lesson. Nothing is published automatically.

## What the AI does

- Gathers material from the sources and suggests points for me to choose from.
- Drafts the short explanations between quotations, which I edit.
- Builds the animations and the quiz from the lesson I approve.
- Handles the technical work of putting each lesson on this site.

## Why every point is a direct quotation

Each point in a lesson stands on the exact words of a trustworthy source: Holy Scripture, the *Catechism of the Catholic Church*, or the *Catholic Encyclopedia*. The teaching comes from the Church in its own words. The explanation around it is there to help you understand it, not to replace it.

Before a lesson can be published, a program checks every quotation word for word against the source it came from. If a single word doesn't match, the lesson can't go up until it's fixed.

## The sources

- **Holy Scripture**, quoted from the *New American Bible, Revised Edition*, as published by the United States Conference of Catholic Bishops.
- **The *Catechism of the Catholic Church***, from the Vatican's website.
- **The *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1913).** It is a century old and sometimes dated, so I check its claims against the Catechism.
- **Wikipedia**, for background only. It is never quoted as an authority.

Every lesson lists its sources at the bottom of the page, with links, so you can read them yourself.

## What these lessons are not

These are short introductions, not official catechesis, and they don't carry an imprimatur. They're meant to help you understand what the Church teaches and to send you to the sources. If you find a mistake, please tell me and I'll correct it.
